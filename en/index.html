A different way to organise professional marketing

Professional marketing should not be a privilege of large companies.

Marketing Studio is developing a knowledge-based system designed to make professional marketing methods accessible to small and medium-sized businesses — not by offering another set of isolated tools, but by organising the knowledge and logic behind the work.

Explore the approach ↓Discuss a pilot

A business owner should not need to know in advance which marketing methods, research or specialists are required. The system is being designed to help identify and organise the work from the business problem itself.

01 · The underlying idea

Start with the domain of marketing — not with a single task.

Most specialist tools start with a defined task and provide a workflow for completing it. A consultant brings personal knowledge and experience. A general-purpose AI model can assist with many tasks, but its professional reasoning is not usually represented as a complete, inspectable model of the domain. Marketing Studio is exploring a different starting point.

Individual expertise

Professional knowledge is accumulated through education and experience. Much of it remains with the individual specialist and is difficult to reproduce consistently.

General-purpose AI

Capabilities emerge from model training, weights, context and tools. AI can produce useful answers, but the underlying professional logic is not necessarily explicit or fully traceable.

Task-specific software

Workflows and algorithms are designed around particular tasks. They can be effective within their scope, but each new task may require another tool or workflow.

Marketing Studio starts from the opposite direction: first model the marketing domain and its relationships; then use that model to navigate from different business problems to the professional work they require.
02 · The knowledge architecture

An explicit model of professional marketing knowledge.

The ambition is to represent marketing as a connected professional domain — its concepts, entities, relationships, methods, conditions and decision rules. The model is intended to provide a shared foundation for different operations rather than a collection of disconnected task automations.

Marketing Map

What exists in the domain

An ontology of 13 interconnected marketing areas. It maps key concepts, entities and relationships, providing a structured representation of the marketing domain.

Purpose: make the domain and its connections explicit.
MASTER

How professional work is done

A methodology organised into 21 professional operations, with defined inputs, outputs, conditions, checks, handoffs and rules for returning to earlier work.

Purpose: turn professional methods into structured, verifiable operations.
Execution and knowledge state

How work stays connected

Software mechanisms are being developed to organise research, evidence, hypotheses, contradictions, decisions and dependencies — and to identify what may need review when information changes.

Purpose: preserve the reasoning and evidence behind decisions.
AI is an execution and analytical capability within this architecture. The explicit knowledge model is intended to define the professional structure and logic of the work.
03 · From a business problem to professional work

The user starts with a need. The system should determine the route.

A business owner can enter through a business problem or a self-assessment. They do not have to assemble a marketing toolkit or design the methodology themselves.

1. Business problem or self-assessmentDescribe a challenge or assess current marketing capabilities.
2. Navigate the marketing domainIdentify the relevant areas, concepts, relationships and unknowns.
3. Select and organise operationsDetermine which professional methods and research are needed, and in what sequence.
4. Evaluate evidence and decisionsSeparate facts, hypotheses and conclusions; retain the grounds for decisions.
5. Connect to planning and managementRelate decisions to strategy, plans, budgets, actions and subsequent review.
Illustrative example

“Our sales are falling. What should we do?”

Rather than immediately recommending an advertising campaign, the system is intended to navigate the relevant parts of the marketing domain and identify what needs to be understood first.

The required research and analysis depend on the company's circumstances. Evidence is then connected to possible explanations and decisions. If new information changes an important assumption, the system should identify which related conclusions may need to be reconsidered.

04 · Why this matters

Make professional expertise reusable — while adapting it to each business.

The professional ambition

Separate professional knowledge from dependence on one individual specialist.

Formalised methods and relationships can become reusable across many businesses. The aim is not to give every company the same answer, but to apply a shared professional foundation to each company's own data, market, constraints and objectives.

The economic ambition

Make deeper marketing capabilities accessible to businesses that cannot maintain a full marketing team.

Small businesses often lack not only specialists, but also the capacity to decide which research is necessary, how to interpret it and how to connect findings to action. Marketing Studio aims to make that professional process more accessible and manageable.

05 · Current development stage

A developing system — with a defined methodological foundation.

What exists

The Marketing Map ontology, the MASTER methodology and separate tools for research, strategy, planning and budgeting have been developed to varying degrees. The methodology and its operations are being examined and strengthened through research and practical cases.


What is being built

The integrated software system that connects the knowledge model, professional operations, research evidence, business-specific state and decision revision. Its quality, usability and economic value still need to be validated with businesses.


What we need to prove

That the model can guide real professional work reliably, make its reasoning and evidence more traceable, adapt to different businesses and deliver useful results at a cost that makes sense for smaller companies.

06 · Principles

Methodological depth inside. Clarity and practical value outside.

Evidence before conclusions

Distinguish verified facts, assumptions, hypotheses and interpretations. Make limitations visible.

Connected decisions

Preserve relationships between research, reasoning, strategic choices, plans and actions.

Review when facts change

Identify which assumptions and decisions may be affected by new information, rather than treating old conclusions as permanent.

Professional methods

Use explicit methods, conditions and quality checks instead of relying only on loosely structured prompts.

Business-specific application

Apply shared professional knowledge in the context of each company's market, resources and constraints.

Human judgement matters

Technology supports analysis and execution. Important business decisions still require context, responsibility and human judgement.
